01 / THE PRODUCT

What is it?

A customer sends a benchmark. A competitor publishes a release blog. The natural response is, “Yeah, but that benchmark is total trash.” Professionalism, unfortunately, tends to get in the way.

Leaderboard Arena supplies a more professional response: a link to that benchmark’s position in the standings. An elegant way to signal just how much of a f-ck anyone should give about GSM8K.

02 / THE PROBLEM

Why?

Working at a frontier model company is exhausting. Customers have no idea how your model works, salespeople have no idea what AI is, and VCs have no idea how stacked your post-training setup is. Instead, they all live and die by their leaderboard of choice.

Customer VP doesn’t like the sound of your TTS generation? No worries! They can easily find a third-rate benchmark that backs them up.

Sales guy getting mogged by your competitors? No problem! Apparently, “this model is at the Pareto frontier”—on one very particular benchmark.

THE ACTUAL METHODOLOGY

Their rankings.
Your verdict.

The arena shows a published model ranking: “Artificial Analysis ranks Opus above Fable.” Agree or disagree with that call. Your vote changes the credibility score of the evaluator that published it. Model scores stay as reported by the original source.

Starting Elo scores are editorial takes informed by published methodology and public discussion. These are editorial opinions, not measured public consensus, and they add no community votes. Read the starting scores and sources ↗.

Scores then use an Elo-style update (K = 32) against a fixed 1,200 reference: agreement raises the evaluator’s score; disagreement lowers it. One vote per ranking per browser, with source snapshots kept separate. Connected deployments share their standings; local development without a database saves votes in your browser.
